coldscreen

First-pass deal screening from public sources. One command in, one screening memo out.

Give it a company name. It pulls the public record and produces the memo a corporate finance analyst would write before anyone agrees to a meeting: registry profile, officer and ownership network, sanctions and PEP exposure, adverse media, and a claims-vs-evidence table testing what the company says about itself against what the record actually supports.

It is not due diligence. It is the screen that decides whether due diligence is worth anyone’s time.

01

How it works

Four commitments, enforced in code by schema validation and the rubric gate, not merely stated in a prompt.

Deterministic collection, model synthesis

Code fetches facts. The model reasons over structured facts and drafts prose, but it never supplies a registry fact from its own memory: registry data enters the context as fetched JSON or not at all.

Evidence or it did not happen

Every finding carries a source URL and a retrieval date. A finding without evidence fails schema validation and is dropped. The case directory doubles as an audit pack: memo, casefile, and every raw response.

Absence is data

Empty results are recorded as explicit findings. “No insolvency notices found” is a finding, not silence, and a stage that never ran is written down as exactly that.

Confidence is stated

Every finding carries a confidence tag: confirmed, indicated, or unverified. Memos describe what the public record shows and with what confidence; they do not make accusations.

The pipeline

  1. 01Entity resolutionregistry search, disambiguation
  2. 02Registry passprofile, officers, PSC, filings, charges, insolvency
  3. 03Network expansionofficers’ other appointments, disqualifications
  4. 04Sanctions and PEPOpenSanctions match: entity, officers, PSCs
  5. 05Adverse mediastructured web search, deduplicated, dated
  6. 06Claims extractiondeck and site into discrete, checkable claims
  7. 07Synthesisclaims vs evidence, rubric, verdict, questions
  8. 08Rendermemo plus evidence bundle in the case directory

Stages 1 to 5 are deterministic: HTTP and parsing. Stage 6 uses the model for extraction over provided text. Stage 7 is the only reasoning stage. Stage 8 is templating.

02

What it checks

Five passes, one casefile. Built for people who screen inbound companies for a living: corporate finance advisors, VC and angel associates, corp dev teams, compliance analysts.

I

Registry pass

stages 01 to 02

Company profile, incorporation history, officers, current and recently resigned, persons with significant control, filing history, registered charges, insolvency events. Every raw response is persisted as evidence.

II

Officer network

stage 03

Each officer’s other active appointments and any disqualifications, checked against the disqualified officers register. Undisclosed related-party webs show up here.

III

Sanctions and PEP

stage 04

The entity, its officers, and its PSCs matched against OpenSanctions data, with match scores recorded. Sub-threshold results are written down as “no match above threshold”, with the threshold stated. You bring your own key, under your own licence.

IV

Adverse media

stage 05

Structured search across fraud, insolvency, regulatory, and litigation queries, with sources and publication dates recorded and duplicates removed.

V

Claims vs evidence

stages 06 to 07

Discrete, checkable claims extracted from the pitch deck and website, then tested against the record. This table is the point of the whole exercise. Unfalsifiable puffery is listed, tagged out of scope, and never dropped.

coldscreen screen 01234567 --deck pitch.pdf --site https://example.com

04

Verdict discipline

Verdicts follow a published rubric, version-controlled with the code and cited by trigger ID in every memo, so changing the logic is a visible, reviewable act. This is rubric v0.3 in full.

Rubric rule 4 · the floor and the ceiling

“Mechanically detected triggers cannot be dropped by the model, and triggers whose evidence conditions are not met cannot be added by it. The model’s judgment operates inside those conditions, never on the level arithmetic.”

Every trigger’s evidence condition is published below, and every enforcement intervention is recorded in the casefile and the memo. In cross-model testing, a 30B coder model and a 27B generalist produced identical verdict levels on the same live companies.

RED triggers any one forces a RED verdict

IDTriggerFires only when
R1Sanctions or PEP match (entity or PSC) at or above thresholdA sanctions match finding at or above threshold exists for the entity or a PSC. Officer matches are reported as amber findings, not R1.
R2Active insolvency eventInsolvency cases are on the register, or the company status is itself an insolvency state (administration, liquidation, receivership, insolvency proceedings, voluntary arrangement).
R3Disqualified director in current officer setA strong-match disqualification (name and date of birth, or name and company number for corporate officers) that is currently active.
R4Material claim directly contradicted by registry recordA surviving contradicted claim assessment whose basis includes a registry finding relevant to the claim’s category, or a stored claim whose text places the company’s origin in a calendar year before the registry incorporation date.
R5Undisclosed related-party network across officersCo-appointment overlap findings exist and claims material is present to judge disclosure against.

AMBER triggers at least AMBER; never escalates to RED, however many fire

IDTriggerFires only when
A1Overdue or irregular filingsThe registry marks accounts or the confirmation statement overdue.
A2Wholesale officer changes within 12 monthsResignations in the last 12 months meet the wholesale threshold.
A3Charge stack inconsistent with stated capital storyCharges exist on the register and at least one checkable financials claim was extracted.
A4Central claim unverifiable from any public sourceAt least one checkable claim was assessed unverified by the model itself.
A5Corporate age or scale inconsistent with stated historyAt least one checkable history claim was extracted.
A6Substantive adverse media (confirmed source)The media stage ran and returned at least one item.
A7Company status is not activeThe registered status is dissolved, closed, converted-closed, or removed. Insolvency states escalate to R2 instead.

GREEN

Clean registry, claims supported or plausibly verifiable, no substantive adverse media. A verdict that cites no triggers must be GREEN.

The five rules

  1. Any RED trigger forces a RED verdict.
  2. Any AMBER trigger yields at least AMBER. AMBER triggers never escalate to RED, however many there are.
  3. The verdict block must cite trigger IDs. A verdict that cites no triggers must be GREEN.
  4. Mechanically detected triggers cannot be dropped by the model, and triggers whose evidence conditions are not met cannot be added by it.
  5. A verdict is an opinion generated from public sources at a point in time. It is a reason to ask better questions, not a substitute for judgment.

Because a verdict is a reason to ask better questions, every memo closes with the 3 to 5 clarification questions an advisor would actually send.

05

Run it fully local

Works with hosted models (Anthropic, OpenAI) or local models via Ollama. The deterministic collection layer is identical regardless of model; only synthesis quality varies.

With a hosted model, one document leaves your machine: the synthesis input, meaning findings, officer names and roles, extracted claims, media titles, and sanctions screening summaries. With local Ollama, it does not. Run fully local if your deal flow should never touch a third-party API.

Registry queries still go to Companies House, because that is the record being pulled. Every response is stored on your disk, and the case directories and local HTTP cache are yours to protect and to delete. Erasure means deleting both, and PRIVACY.md states exactly where they live.

coldscreen has no telemetry. Nothing else receives anything.

One practical note for local models: reasoning-family models (qwen3 non-coder variants and similar) need COLDSCREEN_OLLAMA_THINK=false for reliable structured output.

06

Open methodology

The differentiation is the methodology and its provenance, not the plumbing: the rubric, the claims-vs-evidence discipline, and the shape of the memo, all published.

MIT licensed

The code is MIT. Data sources retain their own licences, and the README states exactly how they bind you rather than hinting at it.

Every decision logged

DECISIONS.md is a dated record of every deviation from the spec and every verified fact, from the PDF-library licence check to the DNS-rebinding hole a security review found in the site fetcher and closed.

Adversarially reviewed

Each milestone was built, then reviewed by an independent fresh-context agent, then fixed against the accepted findings. An external review of the whole repository was adjudicated in the open, finding by finding.

Tested to reproduce

469 tests across Python 3.11, 3.12, and 3.13, with network isolation enforced in CI. The golden RED memo replays byte-identically through the real CLI, claims table included.

Type3 Capital is a licensed corporate finance advisory firm. Every inbound mandate goes through the same outside-in screen before we engage: no data room, no management calls, public sources only. In recent months that screen led us to decline two inbound mandates after central marketing claims failed to survive contact with the public record.

This repository is that screen, generalized and automated. A methodology like this gets stronger in public, not weaker, so we open-sourced it.

From the README · where this comes from

Scope, stated plainly v0.1

  • UK companies only. Sweden (Bolagsverket) and US (SEC EDGAR) adapters are planned.
  • No financial statement analysis. Filings are inventoried, not parsed.
  • No people search beyond registered officers and PSCs.
  • No continuous monitoring. One screen, one memo.
  • No data room ingestion. This tool runs before any documents change hands.
  • Public sources only. Nothing behind a login, no scraping in breach of a site’s terms.

Issues and PRs welcome. The highest-value contribution is a registry adapter for your jurisdiction; the interface is small and documented in ARCHITECTURE.md.

A research aid, not due diligence

coldscreen is a research aid. It is not investment advice, not a credit reference, and not a consumer report. Do not use it for decisions regulated under the US Fair Credit Reporting Act (employment, credit, tenancy screening) or equivalent regimes. Officer data is personal data sourced from public registers; you are responsible for processing it lawfully in your jurisdiction. Verify everything independently before acting on it. Memos describe what the public record shows and with what confidence; they do not make accusations.

Data licences

Contains public sector information licensed under the Open Government Licence v3.0. Source: Companies House. Officer data remains personal data under that licence’s carve-out.

OpenSanctions data is licensed CC BY-NC 4.0, and OpenSanctions treats use inside a for-profit business, including compliance screening that earns no revenue, as commercial use requiring a paid licence from them. coldscreen bundles no key: you bring your own key under your own licence relationship, and this repository’s MIT licence does not extend to their data.
